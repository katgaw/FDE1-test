# ACME Analytics Corp — Generative AI Practice (GAIP)

## GAIP Overview and Founding

ACME Analytics Corp launched its Generative AI Practice (`GAIP`) in Q3 2024, responding to surging enterprise demand for production-grade language model systems. Founded as a dedicated service line within the firm — itself established in 2014 by CEO Dr. Priya Ramanathan — `GAIP` was created to consolidate scattered AI pilots into a disciplined, outcome-oriented practice. Leadership recognized that clients struggled to move beyond proof-of-concept demos into reliable, governed deployments. `GAIP` was designed to close that gap. Within its first year, `GAIP` grew to represent roughly a fifth of ACME's new bookings, making it the fastest-growing service line across the firm's global footprint spanning Chicago, London, Singapore, and São Paulo. The practice combines management-consulting rigor with hands-on engineering, reflecting ACME's hybrid heritage as both a strategy advisor and a data implementation partner for mid-market and enterprise organizations.

## GAIP Service Offerings

`GAIP` delivers a focused portfolio of generative AI services rather than open-ended experimentation. Core offerings include retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that ground model outputs in client knowledge bases; autonomous and semi-autonomous agent workflows that automate multi-step business processes; rigorous evaluation frameworks that measure system quality before and after deployment; and fine-tuning advisory that helps clients decide when adapting a base model is worthwhile versus prompting or retrieval alone. Each offering is scoped to deliver measurable business value, not technical novelty. `GAIP` consultants pair domain specialists with engineers so that solutions reflect real workflows. The practice deliberately steers clients away from over-engineering, often recommending retrieval over fine-tuning when knowledge changes frequently. This pragmatic, advisory-first stance distinguishes `GAIP` from pure implementation shops and reinforces ACME's reputation for honest, outcome-aligned guidance.

## The ACME Retrieval Platform (ARP)

The ACME Retrieval Platform, branded `ARP`, is `GAIP`'s flagship internal product and the backbone of most enterprise engagements. For enterprise clients, `ARP` provides secure, governed document search and retrieval-augmented generation over proprietary knowledge bases — contracts, policies, technical manuals, support tickets, and research archives. `ARP` ingests heterogeneous client documents, chunks and indexes them, and serves grounded answers with citations back to source passages, dramatically reducing hallucination risk. Crucially, `ARP` employs hybrid retrieval, combining dense semantic embeddings with sparse keyword (BM25) matching, so that both conceptual questions and exact-term lookups (product codes, clause numbers, acronyms) resolve accurately. `ARP` ships with role-based access controls, audit logging, and tenant isolation suitable for regulated industries. Enterprise clients deploy `ARP` either within ACME's managed environment or inside their own cloud, and `GAIP` engineers configure `ARP` retrieval parameters per knowledge domain.

## How GAIP Engagements Are Priced

`GAIP` follows ACME's firm-wide blended pricing model, combining a predictable retainer with an outcome bonus tied to agreed success metrics. The retainer covers the dedicated team, platform licensing for `ARP` where applicable, and ongoing advisory throughout the engagement. The outcome component aligns ACME's incentives with the client's, paying out when measurable targets are hit — for example, a defined reduction in support handling time, an accuracy threshold on retrieval evaluations, or successful production go-live within a committed window. This blended structure reflects `GAIP`'s confidence in delivering value and reassures clients wary of open-ended AI spending. Engagement sizes vary from short diagnostic sprints to multi-quarter platform builds. By tying a portion of fees to outcomes, `GAIP` discourages scope inflation and keeps both parties focused on business results rather than billable activity, consistent with ACME's broader consulting philosophy.

## GAIP Team and Leadership

`GAIP` is staffed by a cross-functional blend of applied machine-learning engineers, data architects, evaluation specialists, and former industry domain experts who translate business problems into technical scope. The practice draws on ACME's broader pool of roughly 1,180 employees, pulling consultants from analytics and strategy teams as engagements require. Teams are organized into small, senior-heavy pods rather than large pyramids, reflecting the specialized nature of generative AI work. `GAIP` invests heavily in internal enablement, running regular knowledge-sharing sessions on retrieval techniques, evaluation methods, and `ARP` tooling. This keeps the practice current with a fast-moving field. Recruiting emphasizes engineers who can both build robust systems and communicate clearly with non-technical enterprise stakeholders.

## GAIP Evaluation Methodology

Evaluation is central to `GAIP`'s credibility, and the practice treats it as a first-class deliverable rather than an afterthought. For retrieval systems built on `ARP`, `GAIP` measures ranking quality using metrics such as Mean Reciprocal Rank (`MRR`), recall@k, and normalized discounted cumulative gain, quantifying how reliably the right passages surface near the top. On the generation side, `GAIP` assesses faithfulness — whether answers are fully supported by retrieved evidence — alongside answer relevance and completeness, often using a combination of human review and automated LLM-as-judge scoring. `GAIP` builds golden evaluation datasets from real client questions so that metrics reflect actual usage. These evaluations run before launch to set a baseline and continuously afterward to detect regressions. By tying the outcome-bonus portion of pricing to `MRR`, faithfulness, and similar measures, `GAIP` makes quality transparent and accountable to clients.

## Responsible AI and Guardrails

`GAIP` embeds responsible-AI practices throughout every engagement rather than bolting them on at the end. Guardrails begin with grounding: `ARP` returns citations so users can verify claims against source documents, reducing the risk of confident fabrication. The practice implements input and output filtering to block prompt-injection attempts and to prevent disclosure of sensitive or out-of-scope information. Access controls ensure users only retrieve documents they are authorized to see, with full audit logging for compliance review. `GAIP` advises clients on data governance, including how retention, redaction, and consent obligations apply to ingested content. For higher-risk use cases, `GAIP` recommends human-in-the-loop review before actions are taken. The practice also documents model and system limitations transparently, helping enterprise clients deploy generative AI within defensible risk boundaries appropriate to regulated sectors and aligned with emerging governance expectations.

## How GAIP Integrates With the Rest of ACME

`GAIP` does not operate in isolation; it is tightly woven into ACME's broader analytics and strategy services. Many engagements begin in another part of the firm — a data-modernization project or a strategy diagnostic — and surface a generative AI opportunity that `GAIP` then scopes. Conversely, `GAIP` work frequently depends on upstream data engineering that ACME's data teams provide, such as cleaning and consolidating the knowledge sources that `ARP` later indexes. This integration lets ACME offer end-to-end delivery, from strategy through data foundations to deployed AI systems. Shared account leadership across service lines ensures clients experience one firm rather than disconnected teams. The blended pricing model applies consistently across these joint engagements. For ACME, `GAIP`'s rapid growth also pulls demand into adjacent practices, reinforcing the firm's positioning as a single partner for data-driven transformation.

## GAIP Roadmap

Looking ahead, `GAIP`'s roadmap centers on deepening `ARP` capabilities and expanding the practice's evaluation tooling. Near-term priorities include richer multi-modal retrieval so `ARP` can index tables, diagrams, and scanned documents alongside text, and improved agentic workflows that chain retrieval with controlled actions in client systems. `GAIP` also plans to productize its evaluation framework, giving clients self-service dashboards that track `MRR`, faithfulness, and other metrics over time. On the advisory side, the practice intends to publish more anonymized case patterns and reference architectures to accelerate scoping. Geographically, `GAIP` aims to grow delivery capacity across ACME's London, Singapore, and São Paulo offices to serve regional demand and data-residency requirements. Underpinning all of this, `GAIP` will continue investing in responsible-AI guardrails so that scaling capability never outpaces governance, keeping client trust central to the practice's continued expansion.
